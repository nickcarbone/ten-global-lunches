A companion to Ten Weeknights, built to a tighter clock. Five of these use a pan and take fifteen minutes at most. The other five need nothing more than a tin opener, a knife and sometimes a microwave, and are on the plate in under ten. Each is a real midday meal somewhere, and each is built around its protein.

How to read this. Dishes marked no pan are assembled from tins, tubs and the fridge, with a microwave at most. Dishes marked pan or grill go from a cold kitchen to the table inside fifteen minutes. Shopping lists are split into fresh and pantry, and each substitution says how far the swap moves the dish. Five countries carry over from the dinner book, where their lunch traditions are strong enough to earn a second entry.

Japan

Tamago sando

The convenience-shop egg sandwich

Serves 2Active 5 minutesProtein eggsno pan

The sandwich Japanese konbini chains have spent decades refining. Boil the eggs the night before, or in a batch on Sunday, and this is a five-minute lunch. The texture is what makes it: half the egg mashed to a smooth paste, half left in pieces you can see.

Shopping

Fresh

  • 4 eggs, already hard-boiled
  • 4 slices soft white bread, thick cut
  • butter

Pantry

  • 3 tbsp Kewpie mayonnaise
  • 1 tsp milk
  • ½ tsp sugar
  • salt · white pepper

If you don't have it

Kewpie — ordinary mayonnaise with a few drops of rice vinegar and an extra pinch of sugar. Kewpie is made with yolks only and tastes richer, so the copy is close but flatter.

Soft white bread — the fluffy milk-bread style (shokupan) matters more than the brand. A chewy sourdough fights the filling.

More protein — five or six eggs to four slices makes a fatter, sturdier sandwich.

Method

  1. Peel the eggs. Mash two of them to a smooth paste with the mayonnaise, milk, sugar, salt and white pepper.
  2. Chop the other two roughly and fold them through, so pieces of white and yolk are still visible.
  3. Butter the bread right to the edges. It keeps the bread from going damp if you are packing it.
  4. Pile the filling on thickly, close, press gently, trim the crusts as the konbini do, and cut in half.

Boiling, if you must: 9 minutes from boiling water, then straight into cold water. The yolk sets but stays bright.

Türkiye

Menemen

Eggs set softly into peppers and tomato

Serves 2Active 15 minutesProtein eggspan

A breakfast dish in Türkiye that works just as well at one in the afternoon. The eggs go into a thick pepper-and-tomato base and are stirred as little as possible, so they set in soft folds. It is eaten straight from the pan it was cooked in.

Shopping

Fresh

  • 4 eggs
  • 2 long green peppers, or 1 green bell pepper
  • 3 ripe tomatoes, or a 400 g tin
  • bread · parsley

Pantry

  • 1 tbsp butter and a little olive oil
  • ½ tsp Aleppo pepper (pul biber)
  • salt · black pepper

If you don't have it

Sucuk — if you can find the Turkish garlic sausage, slice 60 g and render it in the pan first. It makes a heavier lunch with more protein. Chorizo is the nearest widely available sausage and tastes noticeably Spanish.

Fresh tomatoes — tinned is fine and often better out of season. Fresh ones can be grated on a box grater, skins discarded.

Cheese — a handful of crumbled white cheese or feta stirred in at the end is common.

Method

  1. Chop the peppers small and cook them in the butter and oil over medium heat for 4–5 minutes, until soft.
  2. Add the chopped tomatoes with salt and cook 5 minutes more, until the liquid has cooked off and the mixture is thick.
  3. Turn the heat low. Crack the eggs straight into the pan and leave them for 30 seconds.
  4. Drag a spoon through two or three times, then leave it until the whites are just set and the yolks still loose, about 2 minutes.
  5. Off the heat, scatter Aleppo pepper and parsley. Eat from the pan with bread.

The national argument is over onions: whether menemen should have them at all. This version leaves them out, which also saves five minutes. If you add one, soften it before the peppers.

Mexico

Molletes

Beans and melted cheese on a split roll, with pico de gallo

Serves 2Active 10 minutesProtein beans and cheesegrill

An everyday Mexican standby and the best argument for keeping a tin of refried beans in the cupboard. The beans and cheese do the work; the raw pico de gallo on top keeps it from feeling heavy.

Shopping

Fresh

  • 2 bolillos or crusty rolls, split
  • 150 g Oaxaca cheese, or low-moisture mozzarella
  • 1 tomato · ¼ white onion
  • 1 jalapeño · lime · coriander

Pantry

  • 1 tin refried beans
  • butter · salt

If you don't have it

Refried beans — a tin of black or pinto beans, drained and mashed in a pan with a little of their liquid and a pinch of cumin. Two minutes, and closer than it sounds.

Oaxaca cheese — a stretched-curd cheese, which is why mozzarella stands in well. Cheddar goes greasy here.

More protein — a fried egg on top, or chorizo cooked into the beans.

Method

  1. Heat the grill. Butter the cut sides of the rolls and toast them under it for a minute, until dry and lightly coloured.
  2. Spread the beans thickly over each half. If they come out of the tin stiff, loosen them in a pan with a spoon of water first.
  3. Cover with the cheese and grill 3–4 minutes, until it is fully melted and blistered at the edges.
  4. While they cook, chop the tomato, onion, jalapeño and coriander small and dress with lime and salt.
  5. Spoon the pico over the hot molletes and eat straight away.

Bread matters more than usual. A soft roll goes to mush under the beans. You want a crust that holds.

Lebanon

Balila

Warm chickpeas with garlic, cumin and lemon

Serves 2Active 8 minutesProtein chickpeaspan

A Lebanese breakfast and mezze staple, made in one small pan. Past the chickpeas there are four ingredients, and the dish lives or dies on using enough lemon and enough salt.

Shopping

Fresh

  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 lemon
  • parsley · flatbread

Pantry

  • 1 × 400 g tin chickpeas, liquid kept
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • olive oil · salt
  • 1 tbsp tahini, optional

If you don't have it

Tahini — optional, though a spoonful stirred in makes it richer and adds protein.

Raw garlic — traditional and sharp. If that is too much at midday, warm it in the olive oil for 30 seconds first.

A fuller plate — a spoon of thick yoghurt or labneh on top, or a handful of toasted pine nuts, both common.

Method

  1. Tip the chickpeas into a small pan with a few spoonfuls of their liquid and warm through over medium heat, 3–4 minutes.
  2. Crush the garlic with a good pinch of salt into a paste and stir it in with the cumin.
  3. Crush about a quarter of the chickpeas against the side of the pan so the liquid thickens and clings.
  4. Off the heat, add the juice of half the lemon, a generous pour of olive oil, and salt. Taste again; it will almost certainly want more lemon.
  5. Parsley on top, bread alongside.

Keep the tin liquid. The starchy water thickens into the sauce that holds everything together.

Vietnam

Bánh mì trứng

Fried egg bánh mì

Serves 1Active 10 minutesProtein eggspan

The cheapest bánh mì on the cart and the easiest to make at home, because it needs no cold cuts or pâté. Crisp-edged fried eggs, a slick of mayonnaise, a shake of Maggi, and as much herb and cucumber as the roll will take.

Shopping

Fresh

  • 3 eggs
  • 1 light, crisp baguette
  • ½ cucumber · coriander
  • 1 carrot · 1 red chilli

Pantry

  • mayonnaise
  • Maggi seasoning, or light soy
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar · 1 tbsp sugar
  • oil · salt · black pepper

If you don't have it

The pickle — carrot sitting ten minutes in vinegar, sugar and salt is enough for today. A proper jar of đồ chua keeps a fortnight in the fridge and is worth making on a Sunday.

Maggi — light soy with a pinch of sugar. Maggi is darker and more savoury, and a bottle lasts for years.

Pâté — a smear of coarse liver pâté is the usual base layer on other bánh mì and makes this one more substantial.

Method

  1. Shred the carrot and leave it in the vinegar with the sugar and a pinch of salt while you do everything else.
  2. Fry the eggs in hot oil, hard enough that the whites blister and brown at the edge, yolks still soft. Season well.
  3. Split the baguette, pull out a little of the crumb, and warm it in the oven for a minute if it isn't fresh.
  4. Mayonnaise on both sides, then the eggs, then a shake of Maggi.
  5. Load with drained pickle, cucumber batons, coriander and chilli, and press the roll closed.

Egg count: three eggs in one roll sounds excessive and is right. With two, the bread wins.

Spain

Ensalada de garbanzos

Chickpea salad with tuna, a summer staple of Spanish home cooking

Serves 2Active 5 minutesProtein tuna and chickpeasno pan

Two tins and a knife. What separates a good version from a dull one is the tinned fish, so this is the recipe to spend on: Spanish tuna or bonito packed in olive oil, not the stuff in brine.

Shopping

Fresh

  • ½ red onion
  • 1 tomato, or a handful of cherry tomatoes
  • ½ green pepper · parsley
  • 1 hard-boiled egg, optional

Pantry

  • 1 × 400 g tin chickpeas
  • 2 tins tuna or bonito in olive oil
  • 1 tbsp sherry vinegar
  • olive oil · salt · black pepper

If you don't have it

Sherry vinegar — red wine vinegar is the closest. Sherry vinegar is rounder and slightly nutty.

Tuna in brine — usable. Drain it hard and add an extra tablespoon of olive oil to make up for what the brine took out.

Red onion — slice it thin and rinse under cold water for ten seconds to soften its bite.

Method

  1. Drain and rinse the chickpeas and tip them into a bowl.
  2. Add the tuna with about half its oil, broken into large flakes.
  3. Slice the onion as thinly as you can, dice the tomato and pepper, and fold everything through with the parsley.
  4. Dress with the vinegar, more olive oil, salt and plenty of black pepper. Leave it five minutes if you can. Quartered egg on top.

Better the next day, cold from the fridge. It is one of the few dishes in either book that improves in a lunchbox, so make double.

Egypt

Ful medames

Fava beans crushed with cumin, lemon and oil

Serves 2Active 8 minutesProtein fava beans and eggsno pan

Egypt's national dish, eaten at every hour and sold from big copper pots on the street. From a tin it needs only warming and crushing, and the toppings turn a bowl of beans into lunch.

Shopping

Fresh

  • 2 garlic cloves · 1 lemon
  • 1 tomato · parsley
  • 2 hard-boiled eggs
  • flatbread

Pantry

  • 1 × 400 g tin ful medames (fava beans)
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp tahini
  • olive oil · salt · chilli flakes

If you don't have it

Tinned ful — chickpeas make a decent bowl by the same method, but that is a different dish. Tinned ful is cheap in any Middle Eastern grocer and keeps for years.

Tahini — leave it out and use more olive oil. The bowl gets lighter and slightly less filling.

The eggs — keep them. They are what carries this through to dinner.

Method

  1. Warm the beans with their liquid in a bowl in the microwave, 2 minutes, stirring halfway.
  2. Crush the garlic with salt and stir it in with the cumin and the juice of half the lemon.
  3. Crush about half the beans with a fork, leaving the rest whole, until it is thick and spoonable.
  4. Spread into a shallow bowl. Pour over olive oil and the tahini loosened with a little water.
  5. Top with chopped tomato, parsley, chilli flakes and the halved eggs. Scoop up with bread.

Taste for lemon twice, once when you mix and once at the table. The beans absorb acidity as they sit, and the bowl goes flat without a second squeeze.

Thailand

Larb gai

Minced chicken with lime, fish sauce and toasted rice

Serves 2Active 15 minutesProtein minced chickenpan

From Laos and Thailand's north-eastern Isan region, and the most protein-dense plate in this book. The mince is poached in a splash of water rather than fried, which keeps it loose and lets the dressing soak in.

Shopping

Fresh

  • 400 g minced chicken
  • 2 limes
  • 2 shallots · 2 spring onions
  • mint · coriander · little gem lettuce

Pantry

  • 2–3 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tbsp raw sticky or jasmine rice
  • 1 tsp chilli flakes
  • ½ tsp sugar · rice to serve

If you don't have it

Toasted rice powder (khao khua) — the one thing you cannot skip; its nutty grit is what makes larb larb. Asian grocers sell it ready-made, and a jar lasts months.

Chicken — pork is just as traditional. Crumbled firm tofu takes the dressing well.

Mint — use a full handful. With coriander alone it tastes incomplete.

Method

  1. Toast the raw rice in a dry pan over medium heat, shaking often, until it is the colour of strong tea, 3–4 minutes. Grind to a coarse powder in a mortar or spice grinder.
  2. Put the mince in the same pan with 4 tbsp water and cook over medium heat, breaking it up, 5–6 minutes, until just cooked and still moist.
  3. Take the pan off the heat. Add the fish sauce, the juice of both limes, the sugar and the chilli flakes.
  4. Stir in the sliced shallots and spring onions, the rice powder, and a lot of torn mint and coriander.
  5. Taste. It should be sour first, salty second, hot third. Eat with lettuce leaves and rice.

Dress it off the heat. Boiled lime juice loses its sourness and the whole dish goes dull.

Poland

Twarożek

Curd cheese with radish and chives, on rye

Serves 2Active 5 minutesProtein curd cheeseno pan

What Poland eats when there are five minutes and no intention of cooking; around Poznań the same thing goes by gzik. Twaróg is drier and far higher in protein than cream cheese, which is why this sits like a meal.

Shopping

Fresh

  • 250 g twaróg (farmer's cheese)
  • 3 tbsp soured cream
  • 5–6 radishes
  • chives · spring onion · cucumber
  • dark rye bread

Pantry

  • salt · black pepper
  • butter for the bread

If you don't have it

Twaróg — well-drained cottage cheese, or quark. Both are milder and wetter; twaróg has a clean sourness that the copies only partly catch.

Soured cream — plain yoghurt, which makes it tangier and lighter.

Rye — a dense dark loaf holds the weight. Soft white bread collapses under it.

Method

  1. Break up the twaróg in a bowl with a fork and stir in the soured cream until spreadable but still coarse.
  2. Slice the radishes thinly, chop the chives and spring onion, and fold most of it through with plenty of salt and pepper.
  3. Butter the rye, pile the cheese on, and scatter the rest of the radish and chives on top.

Season harder than feels right. Unsalted curd cheese is bland, and radish and chives can't carry the plate by themselves.

Brazil

Bauru

Roast beef, tomato, pickle and melted cheese in a hollowed roll

Serves 1Active 8 minutesProtein roast beef and cheeseno pan

Created in 1934 at the Ponto Chic bar in São Paulo and named after the law student who ordered it, nicknamed for his home town of Bauru. It is still on lanchonete boards across the country. Two details make it a bauru: the soft crumb is pulled out of the roll, and the cheese is melted separately and spooned in.

Shopping

Fresh

  • 1 pão francês or small crusty roll
  • 120 g sliced roast beef
  • 100 g mozzarella or another mild melting cheese
  • 1 tomato
  • 1 pickled cucumber

Pantry

  • dried oregano
  • salt · black pepper

If you don't have it

Roast beef — ham is the everyday variation across Brazil. The city of Bauru has written the original recipe into municipal law, so by its reckoning a ham version is something else.

The melting — the original melts the cheese in a bain-marie. Thirty to forty seconds in the microwave gets you to the same molten state.

Bigger lunch — double the beef before you double the cheese.

Method

  1. Split the roll and pull out most of the soft crumb from both halves, leaving a shell.
  2. Put the cheese in a small bowl with a teaspoon of water and microwave in short bursts until fully soft and stretchy.
  3. Layer the roast beef into the roll, then sliced tomato and pickle, with salt, pepper and a pinch of oregano.
  4. Spoon the melted cheese over the filling, press the roll closed and eat while the cheese is still soft.

Why hollow the roll: it makes room for the filling, and the crust walls stop the melted cheese running out of the sides.

The shelf

Nearly all of the no-pan half of this book comes out of tins and tubs. Keep these in and lunch stops being a decision.

  • Tinned chickpeas
  • Tinned ful medames (fava beans)
  • Tinned refried beans
  • Good tuna or bonito in olive oil
  • Kewpie mayonnaise
  • Maggi seasoning
  • Fish sauce
  • Tahini
  • Sherry vinegar and rice vinegar
  • Ground cumin, dried oregano, chilli flakes, Aleppo pepper
  • Raw sticky or jasmine rice, for toasting
  • A dozen eggs, half of them boiled on Sunday

Where the clock won

The tamago sando counts as five minutes only because the eggs were boiled earlier. The ful and the balila start from tins; street ful in Cairo is simmered for hours from dried beans, though tinned ful is an ordinary pantry item there too. The bánh mì pickle gets ten minutes here where a proper jar gets a day. And the bauru's cheese is melted in a microwave where the original uses a bain-marie. Everything else is cooked the way it is at home.
